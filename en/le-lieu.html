The place

187 metres above the sea.

At the top of Mont Saint-Clair, in the Pierres Blanches state forest, where the road stops and the view begins.

L’histoire

The end of the road.

Mont Saint-Clair rises 187 metres over Sète. On its western flank, the Pierres Blanches state forest takes its name from the limestone showing through between the Aleppo pines: pale slabs, almost white, laid above the sea. You climb the corniche, cross the scrubland, and the road stops there.

It is at that exact spot, at the end of allée Pierre Barthas, that La Mesa opened — in place of the former Safari, an address many locals will remember. The room was taken over in an Iberian spirit: pale wood, pottery, rattan lanterns, a woven reed canopy to filter the sun, and tables you extend when friends turn up.

“La Mesa” is Spanish for the table. Everything else follows from that: cooking done with care, plates made for the middle of the table, and the idea that you are here to be together first. In front, the view takes in the beaches under the pines, the Mediterranean horizon and, behind, the Thau lagoon.

8h30

Morning coffee

We open early, for those walking up through the forest and those who come to watch the sea wake up. Coffee, fresh juices, pastries, and the snack bar already running.

Midi

The slate

Written each morning from the market and the catch. Mediterranean cooking, fresh local produce: that is the only promise, and it holds every day.

Le soir

The coals and the sun

From April to the end of September, the skewers are made on site in the afternoon and grilled during service. The sun drops straight into the sea, and the lanterns take over.

What makes the house

What we care about.

Fresh and local

The slate follows the market and the day’s catch. Nothing is written in advance: what is good this morning is what will be served at lunch.

Made on site

The skewers are prepared the same day, before service, one by one. It takes longer, and you can taste it.

In the middle of nature

We are guests of a protected state forest. That carries obligations: on waste, on noise, and on what we leave behind.

Here we do not plate up. We set the table.

The team

A small brigade and a dining room that knows its regulars. In high season, when the sunset fills the terrace all at once, service takes a little longer — we would rather say so than have you discover it. We recruit every spring: write to us if you love the sea, service and days that start early.

The rhythm of the year

Three seasons, three houses.

La Mesa lives with the light. What changes from one season to the next, and what never does.

April → September

High season

Open seven days a week, 8.30 am to 11 pm. Breakfast, the slate, snacks all day and skewer evenings until nightfall.

October → end of November

The late season

Morning coffee, the lunch slate and afternoon tea facing the sea. Evenings narrow to Thursday, Friday and Saturday.

December → March

The closing

The house rests for the winter. We reopen with the first sun of spring, on 1ᵉʳ avril.
